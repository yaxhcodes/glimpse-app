# Glimpse: visual language and interaction audit

Date: 6 September 2026. The findings below describe the source and assets at the start of the audit. This is a design and source audit, not a live-device usability study. Existing Android screenshots were reference captures and could lag the source. Motion observations distinguish implementation facts from hypotheses requiring device testing.

## Implemented follow-up

The user selected consistent topic illustrations for every Rediscover card, superseding the personal-thumbnail recommendation below. All 18 topics now have bundled tactile illustrations clipped into Material shapes, beside the title on a solid tonal surface with fixed fine grain. The artwork has no text scrim. Interests shares the grain and shape vocabulary with recognizable Phosphor glyphs. The Home carousel clips fixed-width card content to prevent compressed previews from reflowing their titles.

The shared press response now uses 85 ms touch-down feedback and a velocity-preserving spring release. FABs reuse that primitive; Settings rows receive restrained feedback and theme-derived accents. The press wrapper and loading indicator respect reduced motion. Ask and Search back controls use the shared icon family. Font families, navigation, Interests masonry, theme presets, and data behavior remain as established.

Artwork prompts and provenance are in [rediscover-illustrations.md](rediscover-illustrations.md). Flutter render checks cover the complete illustration library plus light, dark, and AMOLED cards. Focused widget tests cover behavior and enlarged text. No fresh Android APK run or frame-time measurement is claimed. The remaining audit recommendations below are proposals, not completed changes.

## Overall judgment

Glimpse has a strong foundation: Instrument Sans gives the interface character, Newsreader adds an editorial voice, navigation remains compact, and content is generally easy to scan. The shortcoming is inconsistent emphasis. Home and Rediscover lean toward atmospheric editorial cards; Interests looks like a subdued analytical dashboard; Settings uses colorful icon tiles; utility controls mix icon families. They belong to the same product technically, but do not always feel designed together.

The recommended direction is warm, tactile, and recognizable. Give useful content and selected actions stronger visual presence. Let touch, selection, and expansion produce movement. Keep reading surfaces settled. The largest gains will come from artwork, semantic icon consistency, text hierarchy, and a shared motion policy.

Google's Material 3 Expressive research identifies color, shape, size, motion, and containment as ways to communicate function and guide attention. It also documents usability losses when expressive layouts abandon familiar patterns or remove labels. These principles fit Glimpse better than copying system chrome wholesale. [Google Design research](https://design.google/library/expressive-material-design-google-research), [Material expressive guidance](https://m3.material.io/blog/building-with-m3-expressive).

## 1. Rediscover: highest visual priority

**Evidence.** `journey_visual.dart` maps journeys to 18 bundled category artworks. The inspected software and food assets have hazy, grainy textures and weakly recognizable subjects. A substantial gradient scrim further mutes the images. The reference Android captures show large image areas with short, truncated reasons near the bottom. The current screen still uses the same asset pipeline.

**Effect.** Artwork conveys atmosphere more reliably than the actual memory. Reusing the same category artwork makes different journeys look alike. The blurred texture and serif title combination feels contemplative, which works against the desired lively feeling.

**Recommendation.** Replace the category-wallpaper approach as the normal presentation:

- For a single-save memory, use its usable thumbnail or existing entity artwork.
- For a multi-save journey, show one representative image or a restrained composition of two or three existing covers, with consistent cropping. Select from cached/local metadata; do not trigger new enrichment calls for decoration.
- Where imagery is absent or unsuitable, use a clean tonal surface with one recognizable topic symbol and a limited geometric composition. Keep this fallback theme-aware and deterministic.
- Give text a stable, readable surface or a localized scrim. Avoid obscuring the entire image to accommodate text.
- Keep one editorial hero. Make supporting memories more compact, with a complete short reason and recognizable save count. Let text growth determine minimum height at larger font sizes.

Personal imagery can be noisy or low quality. That is the tradeoff: the resolver must reject favicons masquerading as covers, handle failed downloads, and retain a good offline fallback. A more saturated replacement wallpaper alone would not solve recognition.

**Concrete layout defect.** Home uses `CarouselView` with `shrinkExtent: 0`. The reference capture shows the next card's title squeezed into a narrow vertical sliver. Its child text reflows as the item compresses. Keep the inner card at a readable width and clip the peek, or suppress text in the compressed portion. A next-card preview should signal another item without displaying broken text.

Entry points: [artwork](../lib/features/rediscover/journey_visual.dart), [daily cards](../lib/features/rediscover/rediscover_screen.dart), [Home carousel](../lib/features/home/rediscovery_section.dart).

## 2. Interests: recognizable symbols and stronger composition

**Evidence.** Hero and medium cards use procedural patterns, not topic icons. Icons only appear in slim rows. Pattern opacity is approximately 6.6–10.2%, with an additional content-safe fade. The light and AMOLED goldens show extensive low-information space above the titles. Topic chips are 10.5 logical pixels, clamp text scaling to 1.15, and can shrink further through `FittedBox`. The masonry heights vary by save-count thresholds.

**Recommendation.** Give every interest a recognizable symbol in a modest tonal container. Use a small family of shapes—circle, rounded square, gently asymmetric rounded shape—with a consistent glyph weight and optical size. Use a strong tonal fill for the top interest, quieter peer cards, readable 12–13 pixel supporting labels, and fewer tiny pills. Make medium tiles align in a simpler grid; handle the odd final tile as a compact full-width row. This is an intentional alternative to the current masonry composition, worth comparing before implementation.

Preserve the meaningful order: top signal, growing interests, remaining topics. Patterns may remain as a faint supporting detail, but should not carry recognition by themselves. Use theme roles consistently and retain an explicit neutral fallback.

**Semantic defect.** `_iconForCluster` uses raw substring matching. The `ai` needle can match `trails`, for example, if an earlier rule does not catch the topic. The resolver is also largely English-based. Use existing semantic/category metadata where available; otherwise match normalized phrases or token boundaries with locale coverage. Resolve one visual identity and reuse it across the hero, grid, slim row, and detail surface. Do not introduce a new AI request to choose an icon.

The accompanying Interests concept uses the existing golden's illustrative topics. Its icons are visual stand-ins, its expansion text is illustrative, and its browser animation is not a Flutter performance demonstration. The concept explores stronger hierarchy, topic recognition, and a simpler grid.

Entry points: [cards and icon resolver](../lib/features/mindmap/cluster_card.dart), [screen composition](../lib/features/mindmap/mindmap_screen.dart), [patterns](../lib/features/mindmap/cluster_pattern.dart).

## 3. Motion: improve response and continuity

**What exists.** Shared motion tokens, spring-release card scaling, FAB corner morphing, a morphing loader, theme transitions, Material navigation, and Android predictive-back transitions are already implemented. This is not an animation-free app.

**Why it may still feel restrained.** The shared card/FAB press begins with 180 ms of accelerating easing; a quick tap may release before much deformation occurs. Both spring releases restart with zero velocity. Rediscover cards and Settings rows do not use the shared spring wrapper. `springSubtle` is defined but has no call sites in the searched `lib` tree. These are implementation observations; their perceived impact needs live-device comparison.

Suggested motion contract, with values treated as tuning starting points:

| Interaction | Recommended behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Touch down | Immediate state layer plus a short 70–100 ms response. About 1–2% compression on large cards; slightly more on compact actions. |
| Release | One restrained spring settle, continuous from current state and velocity. |
| Selection | Indicator and relevant shape settle together; preserve text clarity. |
| Expand/collapse | Animate the local container's bounds so content has a clear origin. |
| Navigation | Preserve Android predictive back; avoid a second decorative bounce across the whole page. |
| Save completion | Brief local confirmation with a clear state change; no repeated celebration or background pulsing. |
| Colors and opacity | Smooth bounded interpolation, independent of spatial overshoot. |

Unify policy in shared primitives rather than scattering animation wrappers across screens. Consolidate the similar pointer/slop implementations in the card and FAB wrappers. Cancellation during scrolling, multiple pointers, rapid taps, disabled controls, and keyboard focus must remain correct.

**Accessibility gap.** The custom tap/FAB wrappers and repeating loader do not explicitly consult reduced-motion settings. Add an explicit policy: immediate or near-instant state feedback and a static loading representation when motion is disabled. Keep useful loading semantics localized. Do not merely accelerate an infinite animation and call it reduced motion.

The app intentionally suppresses overscroll stretching. Keep that prior behavior unless separately reconsidered; tactile controls do not require restoring whole-page stretch. Blur already exists in shared chrome with sigma 18. Profile existing blur before extending it across more surfaces.

Entry points: [tokens](../lib/shared/theme/app_motion.dart), [tap response](../lib/shared/widgets/expressive_tap_scale.dart), [FAB](../lib/shared/widgets/expressive_fab.dart), [loader](../lib/shared/widgets/expressive_loading_indicator.dart), [glass surface](../lib/shared/widgets/app_glass_surface.dart).

## 4. Icons: one consistent vocabulary

`AppIcons` centralizes regular Phosphor icons and filled selected variants, but many utility screens still use legacy Material outlined/rounded icons. Ask uses an iOS-style back chevron while Search uses an Android arrow. Interests slim rows use Material glyphs alongside Phosphor navigation. The result is uneven stroke density and metaphor.

Keep the established Phosphor family for custom app actions, use filled variants for selection, and normalize optical size and stroke weight. Keep native control indicators and brand logos where appropriate. Material 3 does not require replacing every custom glyph with Material Symbols. A full icon-family migration would be a larger, optional brand decision; mixing another family would make consistency worse.

Centralize back, close, more, external-link, add-note, share, and topic identity first. Keep visible navigation labels. Do not put every utility icon inside a colored container; reserve those containers for grouping, selection, and topic identity.

Entry point: [icon vocabulary](../lib/shared/theme/app_icons.dart).

## 5. Typography: preserve the fonts, strengthen the hierarchy

Instrument Sans and Newsreader are worth retaining. Their roles need tighter boundaries: sans for navigation, controls, counts, and compact browse cards; serif for a few editorial titles and reading content. Rediscover currently uses editorial type for both the introduction and cards, which multiplies emphasis.

Suggested working hierarchy: 28–32 for one expressive screen heading, 18–20 for section headings, 16–18 for card titles, 14–16 for supporting text, and 12–13 for metadata. These are roles to test, not blanket substitutions. Existing small overrides bypass the shared theme, including 10–11 pixel Rediscover metadata and 10.5 pixel Interests chips. URL-card metadata uses `outline`, which should be checked against actual backgrounds rather than assumed to be a safe text role.

Shorten secondary copy before shrinking it. Remove unnecessary chip decoration where plain text scans better. Let cards grow or simplify at large text sizes; avoid preserving geometry by shrinking meaningful labels. Keep sufficient text contrast in light, dark, AMOLED, and dynamic palettes.

Entry points: [type scale and theme](../lib/shared/theme/app_theme.dart), [editorial helper](../lib/shared/theme/app_typography.dart), [URL card](../lib/shared/widgets/url_card.dart).

## 6. Color: improve its application before adding presets

The theme already derives coordinated Material colors with `ColorScheme.fromSeed`, normally using `tonalSpot`; wallpaper-based dynamic color is the default preference. The issue is how little of that palette distinguishes hierarchy on some screens, while Settings uses independent hardcoded colors.

Use neutral or lightly tinted reading surfaces, one clear primary emphasis, secondary containers for supportive controls, and selective tertiary accents for discovery. Make the strongest interest and the most useful action visibly stronger than their neighbors. Increase separation between dark surfaces without making every card saturated. Preserve true-black mode, but ensure cards and controls remain discernible within it.

Replace hardcoded Settings accents with theme-role pairs or deliberately harmonized semantic accents. Preview a real mini card, button, and text hierarchy in Look & feel rather than relying only on a large color strip.

Existing presets include Pink and Sakura. Earlier notes identified their overlap; the current enum still contains both. Compare generated light/dark palettes before consolidating them or adding an earth palette. Persisted enum indices require a compatibility migration if visible choices change. No new palette is approved or colorimetrically validated by this audit.

## 7. Settings: refine prioritization

The current structure is already sound: a large collapsing title, 28-radius grouped containers, 68-pixel minimum rows, clear labels, and substantial controls. Preserve those foundations.

Recommended top-level order: compact account/plan summary; personalization with current values; notifications; library gestures; privacy/data; about. Notifications is a general preference and should be easier to reach than specialized swipe customization. Keep sign-out/account management together. Move Clear all data into Data & backup and account deletion into account management, preserving explicit confirmation and discoverability within those destinations.

Show meaningful current values where practical—for example the selected appearance—instead of subtitles that simply repeat the row title. Reduce singleton groups where they create excessive visual fragmentation. Use color by group, with error tones reserved for destructive actions. Apply restrained local touch feedback to rows; avoid scaling an entire settings group.

This assessment is source-based; no fresh Settings screenshot was captured during this audit.

Entry points: [Settings composition](../lib/features/settings/settings_screen.dart), [shared rows](../lib/features/settings/settings_components.dart), [appearance controls](../lib/features/settings/look_and_feel_screen.dart).

## 8. Navigation and everyday flows

**Home.** Rediscover is the dominant opening feature while Capture is an icon in the upper toolbar and Ask has a labeled FAB. That hierarchy favors revisiting and asking over manual capture. Keep the four labeled bottom destinations initially. Test a clearer labeled Capture entry and a more compact Rediscover preview before adding navigation destinations or competing FABs. Validate one-handed access and that overlay chrome does not obscure the final card.

**Collections and Library.** Automatic Library discovery sits behind the Collections gateway. The distinction between user-created collections and automatically found entities is useful, but discoverability may suffer. First strengthen the gateway's label, preview, and placement. A Library tab containing automatic types and manual Collections is a larger information-architecture hypothesis, requiring task testing rather than a cosmetic rename. Keep Sources origin-only and Interests inferred.

**Ask.** Preserve the current shared composer surface; the checked-in screenshot predates its latest source structure. Standardize the back action and give selected suggestions/attachment changes clear local feedback. Keep allowance information readable without making it the dominant heading. Do not animate every existing message whenever conversation state changes.

**Capture.** The large introductory copy makes the form welcoming but consumes substantial space. On repeat use, test a shorter heading so link entry and the keyboard-visible Capture action dominate. Collection and note remain optional. Preserve immediate saving and graceful enrichment fallback.

**Details.** The editorial heading and content-first layout are strengths. Keep section order and reading density. Make the relevant action—open source, cook, visit, watch—easy to find. Use expansion motion for local sections where useful, while keeping body text stable and readable.

## Delivery order and validation

1. Repair recognition and readability: carousel compression, small labels, icon matching, shared action icons, and reduced-motion behavior.
2. Redesign Rediscover artwork treatment and Interests identity together. Approve light/dark/AMOLED examples using real saves and empty/failed-image cases.
3. Tune shared touch/selection motion on a device, then apply consistently to the intended controls.
4. Refine Settings grouping and color roles, plus the appearance preview.
5. Test navigation alternatives only if users still struggle to find Capture, Library, or Rediscover.

Acceptance checks for implementation: compact phone and tablet; supported locales and large text; screen-reader labels and target sizes; long titles and no-image fallbacks; reduced motion; rapid taps, cancelled presses and scroll gestures; predictive back; cold-launch behavior; and profile/release frame timings on an identified device. Compare frame times against its actual refresh budget, including 60/120 Hz where available. Do not infer smoothness from widget tests or browser concepts.

The current audit inspected source and visual assets. It did not run a fresh Android build, collect frame timings, measure all contrast pairs, or conduct user testing. Those remain validation tasks for implementation, not claimed results.
